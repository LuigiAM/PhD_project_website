MEMoPAD Newsletter · August 2026

Phase VI: taking MEMoPAD out of the lab

Sent on 3 September 2026

Update, 28 September 2026: recruitment for Phase VI is now closed. Thank you to everyone who applied and shared the call.

After two years of newsletters and six phases of co-design, this issue carries the message the whole project has been building towards: Phase VI is open, and we are looking for 10 people to take MEMoPAD home. 🎉

Phase VI

Everything until now has happened in workshops, in labs, and on paper. Now, we are bringing MEMoPAD into real life: a working watch and companion app, used in your own days, for up to five weeks.

Who we’re looking for:

  • Adults living with an anxiety disorder;
  • Based in or able to travel to Bristol, since devices are handed over in person, in a 30-minute session;
  • Comfortable wearing a smartwatch daily and checking in through the app.

You keep the Pixel Watch 2, and there is a £55 voucher after the closing focus group.

👉 More info and applications at this link: https://go.uwe.ac.uk/MEMoPADphaseVIrescheduled

Places are limited to 10! If this isn’t right for you, forwarding this to someone it might suit is just as valuable.

2 -1 Good news

I have joined the UKRI Mental Health Platform as an Associate Member, a network for researchers working outside its main hubs who share its aims and values. It opens collaborations a single PhD project cannot reach on its own.

And MEMoPAD’s co-design story has been accepted for the MHP Research Summit 2026 in Sheffield on 8 October, as both a talk and a poster, selected by an external panel, and that panel included people with lived experience. Not a secondary detail, for a project built on co-design.

And one that didn’t land… MEMoPAD applied to the Primary Care Ready programme by Health Innovation West of England and was not selected. The reason is fair: the real-world evidence isn’t there yet. Phase VI is precisely how that starts to change.

Opportunity alert

Something you might be interested in: JMIR Mental Health has opened a theme issue titled “Does It Work, Does It Translate, and Does It Help or Harm?” on evaluating digital mental health tools. It is open until 31 January 2027, and the editor is John Torous, MD MBI. Worth a look if you work in this space. 👀 https://mental.jmir.org/announcements/726

The end of the PhD is approaching, this is the last sprint of a long marathon. Thank you to everyone who has supported the project so far. Help us spread the word about Phase VI, I’m sure you’ll love what we’ve developed!
