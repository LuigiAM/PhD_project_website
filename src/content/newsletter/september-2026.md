September was the month MEMoPAD left the lab. After years of workshops, surveys and prototypes, it is now on people's wrists, in their ordinary days. ⌚

## Phase VI is under way

Ten people from our patient group are now using the latest MEMoPAD prototype, a smartwatch and its companion app, for up to five weeks. All ten places were filled, and the watches were handed over in person in Bristol in mid-September.

Why does this matter? It is the first time MEMoPAD is being used in everyday life rather than in a workshop or a lab. It will show us whether a tool designed together with the people it is for is one they actually keep using.

What comes next:

- **Late October:** online focus groups with the ten participants, to hear what worked and what didn't.
- **30 October to 10 November:** 15-minute online interviews with clinicians, to review whether what MEMoPAD produces would be useful in clinical practice.

If you are a clinician who signed up through our recruitment survey, an invitation is on its way to your inbox.

A huge thank you to everyone who applied to take part in this phase, and to everyone who shared the call. We deeply value your support and involvement. 💚

## Behind the scenes

Before any watch could be handed over, the prototype went through a review by UWE's Cyber Security team, on top of its ethics approval. It was demanding, and it is the reason the start date moved a little later than planned. The result is a system that handles participants' data with the care it deserves. Thank you to the UWE colleagues who reviewed it, and to my supervisory team for their steady guidance.

## Your turn: one last survey

The project is not over yet. In November, if you signed up to MEMoPAD through our recruitment form, you will receive a short final online survey.

- **If you took part**, tell us what you liked and what you would have done differently across this long co-design journey.
- **If you signed up but couldn't take part**, tell us what got in the way.

Critical comments are the most useful kind. Your answers will inform future co-design studies, so that research becomes more and more inclusive of the public's needs and perspectives.
